# Client email

## Purpose

Check an email to a client or prospect before it is sent: it says what the author means, the client knows what to do next, and nothing in it creates a commitment, a misunderstanding or an embarrassment.

## What to check

- The ask: what the client should do, by when, is clear in the first lines.
- Facts: names, company names, dates, amounts, links and attachments mentioned are correct and consistent with earlier messages if given.
- Commitments: no promise of dates, prices, results or scope the author has not agreed to. Dates that depend on the client say so.
- Tone: fits the relationship and the situation (first contact, follow-up, bad news, reminder). Not too familiar, not defensive, no blame.
- Confidentiality: no information about other clients, internal discussions or anything forwarded by mistake in the quoted thread.
- Length: the client can read it in under a minute; details go below the ask or in an attachment.
- Language: matches the client's language and register (for example formal Slovenian with "vi").

## Out of scope

- Personal word choice that does not change meaning or tone.

## Impact anchors

- critical: the wrong recipient's data, a wrong price or a commitment the author cannot keep.
- high: a wrong fact the client acts on (date, amount, link), a tone that damages the relationship.
- medium: an unclear ask, a missing deadline, too long to act on.
- low: style.
