# LinkedIn post

## Purpose

Check a LinkedIn post before it is published: the first lines make the right reader stop, the post makes one point well, and every claim holds up in public.

## What to check

- Hook: the first two lines (before "see more") say why the reader should care, without clickbait.
- One point: the post makes one clear point; everything else supports it or goes.
- Claims: numbers, results, client names and quotes are true, current and allowed to be public. Client names need the client's consent.
- Detail: a post is not documentation. A default, an exception or an option left out is low at most, as long as what the post says stays true for a reader who tries it as written.
- Credibility: no overstatement, no claims of expertise the author's track record does not support.
- Reader: written for the intended reader (buyers, peers, hiring managers), in their words.
- Ending: a clear takeaway or a question; a call to action only if it fits.
- Form: short paragraphs, readable on a phone; hashtags few and relevant; links and mentions correct.
- Voice: consistent with how the author writes elsewhere, if examples are given.

## Out of scope

- Personal taste in style, as long as it is clear.

## Impact anchors

- critical: confidential information, a false claim about a client, a name used without consent.
- high: a claim that is wrong or cannot be backed up, a statement that could be read as offensive.
- medium: a weak hook, more than one point, the wrong reader.
- low: style; a detail left out that a reader would want but that does not make the post wrong.
